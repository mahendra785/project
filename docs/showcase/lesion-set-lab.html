Why equalization helps

A slice through one scan: a confident large lesion and a faint small one. Thresholding has to drop its cutoff below the faint lesion, and the background comes in with it. Equalization rescales each candidate against its own peak, so one cutoff finds both.

What the real experiments found

Pre-registered runs, 3 seeds × 1000 splits, CPU random-forest backbone. These are recorded results, not computed live.

Every method met its guarantee. Joint lesion control cost at most 0.7% over pixel-only control and beat Learn-Then-Test and morphological sets. On these data lesion-equalized sets tied thresholding, because the pixel constraint binds and the backbone is weak.

How this demo works: each scan is 64×64. Lesions are Gaussian bumps with disc-shaped ground truth, plus false-candidate noise bumps. The 300 scans are split 60 / 120 / 120 into tune, calibrate and test. Thresholding is calibrated on tune + calibrate. The lesion-equalized family is picked on tune from 9 candidates and calibrated on calibrate only. Thresholds come from split conformal risk control, (n·R̂ + 1)/(n + 1) ≤ α, on a 201-point grid, taking the largest threshold across the three risks. A lesion is found when at least 50% of it is in the set. Small means below the 33rd percentile of lesion areas.
