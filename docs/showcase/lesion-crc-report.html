Pixel guarantees hide missed lesions

Lesion-level joint conformal risk control, and a prediction-set family built for it. This report covers what was built, how it was tested, what held up and what did not.

Mahendra Sajjan Choudhary (23BCE0701) · Vansh Bhatiya (23BCE0631) · Guide: Dr. Shashank Mouli Satapathy · Branch research/mg-crc · October 2026

Is it novel?

Partly, and in a specific way. A literature search on 14 October 2026 covered arXiv and the web from 2021 onward, using twelve query strings. It found no method that does any of the following three things.

NewLesion-level risk

A guarantee stated on ground-truth lesions (connected components), plus a separate one for small lesions. Every published segmentation method bounds pixels or voxels.

New, but simpleJoint control

Pixel, lesion and small-lesion risk controlled simultaneously in expectation. Once posed, the proof is a short corollary of monotonicity. It is a new formulation, not deep theory.

New, unproven on real dataLesion-equalized sets

A nested set family designed for a lesion loss. It works in simulation (up to 47% smaller sets) but tied the baseline on both real datasets.

What can be claimed. At the same lesion-level guarantee, the joint formulation needs smaller sets than Learn-Then-Test and smaller sets than morphological prediction sets. It costs at most over pixel-only control.

What cannot be claimed. The new set family has not been shown to beat the strongest baseline on real data. The search was thorough but not exhaustive, so a reviewer may still surface related work. The fallback position (contributions 1 and 2 plus the measured gap) holds either way.

The problem

Conformal risk control (CRC) picks a threshold on held-out data so that a segmentation model's expected pixel false-negative rate stays below a level α. The guarantee is exact at finite sample size and needs only exchangeability.

The unit of clinical harm is usually a lesion, not a pixel. A missed 4 mm metastasis next to a well-segmented 4 cm primary barely moves the pixel FNR. Large lesions carry the pixel average, so a pixel guarantee says nothing about small ones.

This is measurable. At the pixel-CRC threshold on Kvasir-SEG, images with two or more polyps miss lesions at × the target rate (α = 0.10), even though the pixel guarantee holds.

Method

Losses on lesions

A segmenter gives a posterior p̂(u) over pixels. The ground truth Y splits into connected components Y¹ … Yᴷ, which are the lesions. A prediction set is Sλ = {u : s(u) ≥ 1 − λ} for a fixed score map s. A lesion counts as found when at least τ = 50% of its pixels are inside the set.

Lpix(λ) = 1 − |Sλ ∩ Y| / |Y|   ·   Lles(λ) = (1/K) · #{k : |Sλ ∩ Yᵏ| < τ|Yᵏ|}   ·   Lsmall = Lles over small lesions only

"Small" means below the 33rd percentile of all lesion areas in the dataset. This is a label-only definition, fixed before testing.

Joint threshold

Run CRC once per risk and take the largest threshold, λ̂∪ = maxr λ̂r. Every risk is then at most α in expectation, simultaneously. A max-form variant applies CRC to the per-image worst of the risks. It gives a strictly stronger guarantee at a larger threshold.

Lesion-equalized prediction sets

Under thresholding (s = p̂), a faint lesion is found only if the global cutoff drops below its confidence. Every background pixel above that low cutoff then joins every set. The new family rescales each candidate lesion against its own peak:

s(u) = p̂(u) / Pjρ   inside candidate Cj of {p̂ ≥ t₀}, with peak Pj   ·   s(u) = p̂(u)   elsewhere   ·   ρ ∈ [0, 1]

The pair (ρ, t₀) is chosen on a tuning split, from a grid that includes ρ = 0, which is plain thresholding. Calibration then runs on a disjoint split, so the guarantee holds exactly.

Theory

Lemma 1 · monotonicity

If the sets are nested and L = f(Sλ ∩ Y) with f non-increasing under inclusion, then L is non-increasing in λ. All three losses qualify, under every family.

Lemma 2 · one number per lesion

Lesion k is found at cutoff c exactly when its ⌈τ|Yᵏ|⌉-th largest score is at least c. The lesion loss therefore costs one sort per image, the same as the pixel loss. Checked against brute force in the test suite.

Proposition 1 · validity with a tuned family

For any rule that picks the family from a tuning split disjoint from calibration, each risk satisfies E[Lr(λ̂∪)] ≤ α. Conditionally on the tuning data, the transform is fixed. CRC applies to each risk, and monotonicity carries the bound to the larger threshold.

Proposition 2 · amplitude invariance

If on a candidate p̂ = a·g, then at ρ = 1 the score equals g / max g. Whether a lesion is found no longer depends on the model's confidence a, only on the shape of its response.

Proposition 3 · background exclusion

For any cutoff at or above t₀, the set lies inside the candidates. Thresholding has no such containment. This is a sufficient mechanism, not an optimality result. The best nested family for a lesion loss is an open question.

Protocol

The hypotheses, metric, splits, grids and decision rules were committed to git before any result existed (docs/research/preregistration-mgcrc.md). Two dated amendments came before the results they affect. Amendment 1 records the switch to a CPU backbone after the downloads were stopped. Amendment 2 adds the simulation and discloses two debug runs seen beforehand.

Datasets
FracAtlas, 719 fractured radiographs (polygons → components). Kvasir-SEG, 1000 polyp images (the single-lesion control).
Backbone
Random forest over a multiscale filter bank, 5-fold cross-fitting. Every image gets a posterior from a model that never saw it.
Repetitions
3 training seeds × 1000 random tune / cal / test partitions (20 / 40 / 40%).
Fairness
Baselines calibrate on tune ∪ cal, which is 50% more data than our method gets.
Metric
Set-to-truth ratio Σ|S| / Σ|Y| on test images, at a threshold covering all three risks. Lower is better.
H1
Pixel CRC under-protects multi-lesion or small lesions. PROCEED if G ≥ 1.5 at α = 0.10; KILL if ≤ 1.1 everywhere.
H2
Lesion-equalized sets are smaller than joint thresholding: mean change below 0, and smaller in at least 95% of splits.

Reading the results

Every guarantee held. On these data the pixel constraint is the one that binds, so adding lesion-level control is nearly free. For the same reason, lesion-equalized sets have nothing to save, and the tuning split often picks plain thresholding.

FracAtlas numbers describe a segmenter that almost fails (out-of-fold Dice ). Kvasir is the more informative dataset (Dice ).

Verdict: (pre-registered rule)

The rule required a gain at every γ ≥ 1 that grows with γ. Instead the gain peaks at γ = 0.5–1 (up to ) and disappears at γ ≥ 1.5. The diagnostic explains this. By then most small lesions do not stand above the background, and no transform of p̂ alone can separate them. Proposition 3's precondition fails, and the method falls back to thresholding instead of doing worse.

Post-hoc analysis: protecting multi-lesion images

This analysis was not pre-registered. Even under the joint guarantee, multi-lesion images still miss lesions above α, because the lesion guarantee averages over all images. A lesion loss restricted to images with K ≥ 2 is valid for CRC for the same reason as the small-lesion loss: it lives on a subpopulation defined by labels.

Limitations

  • The backbone is weak. FracAtlas results describe a near-failing segmenter, so H2 has not been tested where it is most plausible: a strong model whose remaining misses are faint small lesions.
  • Cross-fitted posteriors come from five models, so exchangeability holds only approximately.
  • Neither dataset has patient identifiers, so splits are image-wise.
  • Few multi-lesion images per calibration split (about 29 to 95), so the gap intervals are wide.
  • Guarantees are marginal and conditional on a lesion being present. Nothing here is clinically validated.

What decides it next

  1. Run the GPU U-Net backbone on FracAtlas and Kvasir. The code is ready; it needs a PyTorch install.
  2. Run 3-D MSD Task08 liver tumours, with volumes as units and 26-connected lesions. The pipeline is built; it needs the 9.35 GB download.
  3. Offer the conditional multi-lesion risk as a standard option.
  4. Use weighted conformal to restore validity under the shift that PUMS already detects.
