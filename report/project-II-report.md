## Abstract

Conformal risk control (CRC) guarantees that a segmentation model's expected
pixel false-negative rate stays below a chosen level α. That guarantee is carried
by large lesions and says nothing about whether small lesions are found. This
project (1) defines lesion-miss and small-lesion-miss risks on ground-truth
connected components and controls them **simultaneously** with pixel FNR, and
(2) proposes **lesion-equalized prediction sets**, a new nested set family that
peak-normalises each candidate lesion, so that faint lesions no longer drag the
global threshold down. A pre-registered evaluation used two public datasets
(FracAtlas, Kvasir-SEG), six baselines, 3 seeds × 1000 random splits, and a
controlled simulation.

Four findings stand out. Every guarantee held on held-out data. Pixel-only CRC
left multi-lesion images missing lesions at up to **1.56α** (Kvasir). Joint
lesion-level control cost **≤ 0.7%** in set size, and needed **10–46% smaller**
sets than Learn-Then-Test and **1–43% smaller** sets than morphological
prediction sets. The pre-registered claim that lesion-equalized sets beat joint
thresholding on real data was **not supported**: they tied. In simulation they
cut set size by up to **47%** when small lesions are moderately under-confident,
and fell back to thresholding otherwise.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background
Project-I built PUMS: a model-agnostic, post-hoc layer that adds calibrated,
hierarchical uncertainty, shift detection and a transparent risk signal on top
of any dense medical predictor. Its calibration core is split CRC with a pixel-FNR
loss (Angelopoulos et al., ICLR 2024).

### 1.2 Motivation
In clinical reading, the unit of harm is a **missed lesion**, not a missed pixel.
A 4 mm metastasis next to a 4 cm primary contributes almost nothing to pixel FNR.
The Project-I Week-1 pilot on Kvasir already hinted that pixel CRC under-protects
images with several lesions. Project-II tests this properly and builds a remedy.

### 1.3 Scope
The scope is post-hoc calibration only. No new segmentation architecture is
proposed. Results are research measurements, not clinical claims.

## 3. Objectives

1. Formalise lesion-level and small-lesion risks and prove they are CRC-valid under any nested family.
2. Control them jointly with pixel FNR (union and max-form thresholds).
3. Design a more efficient nested family (lesion-equalized sets) without losing validity.
4. Measure the pixel-to-lesion gap and compare against six baselines under a pre-registered protocol.
5. Integrate the method into the PUMS API and dashboard with full provenance.

## 4. Method

See `paper/sections/method.tex` and `docs/research/theory-memo-v2.md`; proofs are in the paper appendix.

- **Losses:** pixel FNR, lesion miss (a GT component is found iff ≥ τ = 50% of
  its pixels are in the set) and small-lesion miss (components below the 33rd
  size percentile).
- **Joint threshold:** λ̂∪ = max over risks of each CRC threshold. Every risk is
  then ≤ α in expectation, with no Bonferroni split.
- **Lesion-equalized scores:** inside each candidate component of {p̂ ≥ t₀},
  divide p̂ by (component peak)^ρ. This has three properties:
  - it is amplitude-invariant at ρ = 1;
  - it excludes background above t₀;
  - ρ = 0 recovers thresholding.
- **Validity:** (ρ, t₀) is chosen on a separate tuning split, which keeps the
  guarantee exact (Proposition 1).

**Simulation (E7).** The set-size change from lesion-equalized sets was −1% to
−4% at γ = 0 and −20% to −47% at γ = 0.5–1. At γ ≥ 1.5 it was about 0; there,
small lesions are indistinguishable from background (post-hoc diagnostic). The
pre-registered rule required gains at all γ ≥ 1, so the verdict is FAILED.

**Post-hoc (E8).** Adding a risk conditioned on multi-lesion images halves their
miss rate on Kvasir (0.155 → 0.076 at α = 0.10), for 16–28% larger sets.

## 7. Discussion

- **Novel and supported:** the lesion-level joint formulation, its validity, the
  measured gap, near-zero cost relative to pixel CRC, and clear efficiency over
  Learn-Then-Test and morphological sets.
- **Novel but not yet supported on real data:** the lesion-equalized family's
  efficiency gain. The theory says it helps only when the lesion constraint binds
  and small lesions are under-confident yet above background. With the CPU
  backbone, the pixel constraint binds instead.
- **What would decide it:** the GPU U-Net and 3-D MSD Task08 runs, both
  implemented (`--backbone unet-r34`, `train_backbone_msd.py`). They were not run
  because the PyTorch and Task08 downloads were stopped for this round.

## 8. Conclusion and Future Work

Pixel-level guarantees hide missed lesions. Lesion-level guarantees are cheap to
add and better than the existing multi-risk alternatives. The proposed set family
works in the regime its theory identifies. Future work:
1. strong backbones (U-Net, nnU-Net, MedSAM) and 3-D Task08;
2. a conditional multi-lesion risk as a first-class option;
3. weighted conformal to restore validity under the distribution shift that PUMS already detects.
